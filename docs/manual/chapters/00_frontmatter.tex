\chapter*{About this manual}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About this manual}
Doug records financial information supplied by its user and uses that information to describe history and estimate future outcomes. The purpose of this manual is to explain how to perform those tasks, what each result means, and which records or assumptions should be checked when the result is unexpected. Prior knowledge of accounting, statistics, or the application is not required.

The first part provides a starting path and the conventions used throughout the application. The task chapters then explain individual activities, including importing statements, locating goal savings, building a monthly budget, and comparing forecasts. Worked examples connect these activities with amounts that can be checked independently. The calculation chapters develop the underlying methods for readers who want to understand why the application produces a particular result. Appendix D describes how to keep the manual aligned with the implementation.

\begin{notebox}{Scope of this edition}
This edition describes Doug \AppVersion\ and the working implementation inspected on \ExampleDate. GUI review was in progress during preparation. It follows the grouped navigation shown in Chapter~\ref{ch:conventions}; older installed releases may place the same pages directly in the sidebar. A source commit alone does not identify uncommitted GUI work. The recorded example manifest therefore includes the SHA-256 hashes of the inspected application files.
\end{notebox}

The text uses dollars because that is the currency convention implemented by the inspected application. It does not describe currency conversion or independent ledgers in multiple currencies. Figures and worked records are fictional. The diagrams illustrate relationships without depending on the exact position or color of a control in a transient GUI layout.

\section*{Interpretation of results}
A forecast is conditional on the records, assumptions, and model used to construct it. Missing transactions, changed income, unrecorded commitments, unusual expenses, and different investment returns can produce outcomes outside the displayed bands. A data-quality check identifies records to inspect; passing it does not establish completeness or forecast accuracy. A spending score is a benchmark calculation rather than an assessment of a person's circumstances. The manual explains the program's behavior and does not prescribe a financial decision.
